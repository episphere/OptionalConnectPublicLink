Overview

The Connect for Cancer Prevention Study is a large-scale, long-term prospective cohort study designed to create a foundational research resource for studying cancer development in the United States. Funded by the National Cancer Institute (NCI), Connect recruits adults aged 35–70 who are members of integrated health systems across the country.

By enrolling participants before they develop cancer and following them over decades, Connect enables researchers to examine the full spectrum of cancer risk factors — including genetics, lifestyle, environment, and healthcare access — in a real-world setting. The study's scale and diversity make it uniquely powerful for studying cancers that disproportionately affect underrepresented communities.

Connect is not a clinical trial. Participants do not receive experimental treatments. The study collects data and biospecimens over time to build a research resource available to the scientific community.

Study Goals

Connect aims to answer fundamental questions that have remained difficult to study at scale:

  • What biological, behavioral, and environmental factors increase or decrease the risk of developing cancer?
  • Can blood-based and other biomarkers detect cancer years before clinical diagnosis?
  • How do social determinants of health — including income, education, and neighborhood — affect cancer risk and outcomes?
  • Why do certain cancers disproportionately affect specific racial and ethnic groups?
  • What roles do the microbiome, metabolomics, and epigenetics play in cancer development?

These questions are addressed through the integration of survey data, biospecimens, electronic health records (EHR), and passive data collection including wearable devices.

Study Design

Recruitment

Participants are recruited through eight integrated health systems via electronic outreach (email, patient portal) and in-person contact at clinical sites. Enrollment targets 200,000 participants with intentional diversity across race, ethnicity, geography, and socioeconomic status.

Data Collection

At enrollment and periodically thereafter, participants complete online surveys covering diet, physical activity, exposures, medical history, and more. Blood, urine, and saliva samples are collected at clinical sites and stored in a central biorepository.

EHR Linkage

With participant consent, Connect links survey and biospecimen data to electronic health records, enabling longitudinal tracking of diagnoses, medications, labs, and outcomes without requiring additional participant burden.

Follow-up

Participants are followed for 10+ years through annual surveys, EHR data, and cancer registry linkage. Incident cancer cases trigger additional data collection and biospecimen requests to support case-control analyses.

Eligibility

To be eligible for Connect, individuals must:

  • Be between the ages of 35 and 70 at enrollment
  • Be a member of one of the eight participating health systems for at least 12 months
  • Have no prior cancer diagnosis (excluding non-melanoma skin cancer)
  • Be willing to provide blood, urine, and saliva samples
  • Have access to a device with internet connectivity to complete surveys

Eligibility is determined at the health system level. Researchers interested in the cohort's demographic composition can review summary statistics in the Data & Platforms section.

Timeline

2017–2019

Pilot phase at three health systems. Protocol refinement and feasibility assessment.

2020

Full study launch across all eight health system partners. Enrollment opens.

2021–2023

Rapid enrollment growth. Biospecimen collection and initial data releases to research community.

2024–2025

Enrollment target of 200,000 participants reached. Annual follow-up surveys and EHR updates ongoing.

2026 and beyond

Long-term follow-up phase. Expanding research use cases including multi-omic analyses and cancer registry linkage.

Team & Leadership

Connect is led by NCI's Division of Cancer Epidemiology and Genetics (DCEG) and implemented through a coordinating center and a consortium of health system partners.
